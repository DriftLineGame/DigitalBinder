What we're observing with

Two instruments, two very different jobs. One is already collecting light. The other is a proposal in progress.

In use

Seestar S30 Pro

30mm apochromatic refractor · f/5.3 · dual 4K sensors

Our wide-field workhorse. Used for deep-sky imaging now, and for our first research target: a full brightness curve of the eclipsing binary Algol, captured frame by frame and reduced in Siril.

Proposed

Celestron RASA 8"

203mm aperture · f/2 · 400mm focal length

On loan from a club member, if approved. Enough light-gathering power to attempt something bigger: catching the brightness dip of a known exoplanet as it crosses its star.

How a light curve gets made

Every observation follows the same four steps, whether the target is a variable star or a transiting planet.

01

Capture

Hundreds of short, unfiltered exposures taken back to back across the full event — no stacking, so every frame keeps its own timestamp.

02

Calibrate

Frames are corrected for sensor noise and synced to an accurate clock before anything gets measured.

03

Measure

Siril tracks the target star against several steady comparison stars in the same field, frame by frame.

04

Confirm

The resulting brightness-over-time plot gets checked against the published prediction — the dip either shows up, or it doesn't.
